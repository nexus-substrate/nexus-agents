Async jobs allowed past the standard MCP ceiling now have to prove they are alive, and every in-tree async body does. `runAsJob` hands each job body a fourth argument, `progress()`, a heartbeat that stamps `lastProgressAt` on the job record (carried onto the terminal record, so a settled job still says when it last moved); `get_job_result` and `list_jobs` return that field (absent when the body never sent one) so a poller can tell a slow job from a stuck one under either `NEXUS_JOB_RESULT_SOURCE`. When the `async-job-body` guard is raised above `3600000` ms (`NEXUS_TIMEOUT_CLASS_ASYNC_JOB_BODY_MS` or `NEXUS_TIMEOUT_MULTIPLIER`, #6159), a reaper reads the record once per guard / 8 and fails a body silent for three intervals — 3/8 of the guard, measured from the last heartbeat or from job start when there was none — recording `failed` with `wedged (no progress for N ms)` and releasing its concurrency slot before the runaway guard would. Which bodies heartbeat, and how: `consensus_vote`, `pr_review` and `supply_chain_tradeoff_panel` per settled seat (the launcher's new `onVoteCollected`, threaded through `collectRealVotes` / `executeVoting`); `run_pipeline`, `run_dev_pipeline`, `run` and `orchestrate` through the `stage.*` / `model.called` / `pipeline.*` events their stages already emit on the pipeline bus, attributed to the job by the async context they are emitted from (the audit template's stages now emit stage events too); `run`'s consensus strategy per seat; `run_graph_workflow` and `execute_spec` per graph event; `run_workflow` per settled phase (`IWorkflowEngine.execute` gains an optional `onPhaseComplete`). A custom body dispatched through `runAsJob` under a long guard must call `progress()` (or `heartbeatJob(jobId)`) itself, or emit on the pipeline bus — one that never does is wedged by definition. Jobs at or under the standard ceiling are unaffected, and existing two- and three-argument `run` callbacks keep working (#6162, item 1).
